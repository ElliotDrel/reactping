Privacy

Privacy notice

Plain description of the data ReactPing handles.

Draft for the pilot. This describes how ReactPing works today. It has not had a legal review and will be finalized before public launch.

What ReactPing stores

For each person who adds ReactPing in a Slack workspace, one record containing:

  • the Slack workspace ID and your Slack user ID, which are your only identity in ReactPing;
  • the workspace's Slack address (for example https://example.slack.com/), used to link back to messages;
  • the ReactPing bot's access token for that workspace and your own access token, both encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM);
  • when the record was created and last updated.

There is no separate ReactPing account, password, or email address.

What ReactPing never stores

Message text, files, channel history, names, email addresses, or any record of reactions. When Slack reports a reaction to your message, ReactPing turns it into a DM and keeps nothing about it afterwards. The DM itself lives in your Slack, like any other message. ReactPing has no analytics, advertising, or tracking scripts, and does not sell or share data.

How it is used

Only to send you reaction notifications in the same workspace, show your status in ReactPing's Slack Home tab, and keep the service secure. Notifications never move between workspaces.

Browser storage

While you add ReactPing to Slack, the site sets one short-lived, signed, HttpOnly cookie that ties the approval to your browser. It expires after 10 minutes and is cleared when you return. The website uses no other cookies and no scripts.

Reliability

Notifications are best effort. ReactPing does not queue or retry them: if Slack cannot reach ReactPing, or a DM fails, that notification is lost. Reactions from before you connected, or during an outage, are never sent later.

Service providers

  • Vercel hosts the website and the Slack endpoints.
  • Supabase hosts the Postgres database that holds the records above.
  • Slack delivers reaction events and the DMs ReactPing sends.

Hosting providers keep their own operational logs, such as IP addresses and request times. ReactPing's own logs contain workspace IDs and error codes only, never tokens, authorization codes, or message content.

Leaving and deletion

  • Press Disconnect in ReactPing's Home tab in Slack: your record is deleted immediately and ReactPing asks Slack to revoke your access token. Other people's connections in the workspace are not affected, so the shared ReactPing bot stays in the workspace until someone removes the app in Slack.
  • Removing the app from a workspace in Slack deletes every ReactPing record for that workspace.
  • If Slack reports that access was revoked, the affected records are deleted.

Contact

ReactPing is in a private pilot and a public support address has not been published yet. Pilot participants can reach the maintainer through the channel they were invited from. Please never send Slack tokens or message contents.
